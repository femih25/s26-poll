Here's what some undergraduates had to say about the deal.
In the Spring 2026 poll, respondents had the option to provide an open-ended response to the question: "Is there anything else you'd like to share about your perspective on this deal?"
Some students who supported the deal cited the restoration of research funding.
"University would've been hurt by a continued loss of funding. Signing the deal was necessary."
A respondent who expressed they "support" the deal

When the deal was made on Nov. 28, the accrued cost in internal funding for frozen research grants had reached about $350 million, according to the Office of the President website.

"At a cost of $40 million a month, this was simply not sustainable," Bienen said in a Q&A video published on the Office of the President's website.

"The situation is a terrible one to be in, but thousands of graduate students at Northwestern rely upon this federal funding for their livelihood. ..."
A respondent who expressed they "strongly support" the deal

In addition to fueling research for labs across campus, research grants also support some stipends for graduate students. Due to decreased cashflow during the freeze, universities across the country have decreased the amount of students they accept into graduate programs.

"The totality of the agreement is disgusting ... . On the other hand, I know people who lost their jobs at Northwestern because of the freeze and others who couldn't continue their important research, like cancer research, because of the lack of funding. ..."
A respondent who expressed they "neither support nor oppose" the deal

NU implemented a faculty and staff hiring freeze two months after the funding freeze was announced and eliminated more than 400 staff positions — half of which were vacant at the time — at the end of July. The hiring freeze ended in January, Bienen said in a Jan. 9 interview with The Daily.

Some students voiced frustration with the concessions made to the Trump administration in this agreement.
"... even I didn't expect the administration to stoop so low as officially adopting the Trump administration's definition of and positions on gender and transness. ..."
A respondent who expressed they "strongly oppose" the deal

The agreement requires the University to follow sex defined as an "immutable biological classification as either male or female" in a January executive order. NU claimed that "no restrictions" were placed on its transgender community.

NU and the Feinberg School of Medicine also agreed not to perform gender-affirming care on minors. According to the Office of the President's website, the University has "never performed gender-affirming surgeries for minors."

"I understand that they needed to protect funding for important research, but basically backing down on all of the points that students fought for during the encampment is wild."
A respondent who expressed they "oppose" the deal

The Deering Meadow Agreement ended the five-day pro-Palestinian encampment. In the resolution, the University agreed to reverse the policies of the agreement, including reestablishing the Advisory Committee on Investment Responsibility and supporting at least one Palestinian visiting faculty member.

This also came alongside federal investigations related to antisemitism on campus, which were resolved as part of this deal.

"I'm upset that the deal quietly terminated the Deering Agreement, delivering a blow to students' freedom of expression and harming the (Middle Eastern and North African) community at Northwestern. ..."
A respondent who expressed they "oppose" the deal

As part of the terms of the Deering Meadow Agreement, NU provided a temporary space for the MENA Student Association and the Muslim-cultural Students Association, which is no longer available following this deal.

According to the Office of the President's website, the University will partner with alumni to "explore off-campus, privately owned locations."

"... I'm also concerned about the harmful implications of this deal for transgender students and DEI programs."
A respondent who expressed they "oppose" the deal

NU agreed to only consider academic and professional merit for its hiring practices as part of the deal. The University also removed its requirement for "diversity statements" for faculty during the hiring process.

"I'm seriously concerned about how this deal is going to impact free speech at Northwestern."
A respondent who expressed they "oppose" the deal

Vice President for Student Affairs Susan Davis released a campus climate survey in an April 7 email to NU students.

The survey was required as part of the deal, and included questions about whether students feel welcome on campus and safe reporting various forms of harassment and discrimination.

Some students believed the University should not have opted for a deal at all.
"Bienen is showing weakness by giving into the administration's demands. In the short term it might seem better, but now the administration knows exactly what and how to play us to get what they want. ..."
A respondent who expressed they "oppose" the deal

Among students who self-reported that they understood the terms of the deal, 33.4% disapproved of Bienen's job performance.

"Universities are supposed to be the hub of resistance in a nation. I'm disappointed that we caved."
A respondent who expressed they "oppose" the deal

NU joined five other higher education institutions, including the University of Pennsylvania and Columbia University, as the sixth university to finalize a deal with the Trump administration.

"We should have sued their asses. Harvard did it. And won."
A respondent who expressed they "oppose" the deal

As of now, Harvard University is the only institution to have brought the Trump administration to court.

In September 2025, a federal judge ruled in Harvard's favor that the $2 billion in federal grants and contracts frozen violated Harvard's First Amendment rights. The majority of funding has since been restored.

Bienen told The Daily in an Oct. 24 interview that he did not want to sue the Trump administration.

"Fighting the federal government is an incredibly difficult thing to do," Bienen said. "In law, any other way — it's very expensive."

"... I am worried about what precedent signing this deal sets for the future of academic freedom at Northwestern."
A respondent who expressed they "oppose" the deal

The deal will remain in effect for three years.
